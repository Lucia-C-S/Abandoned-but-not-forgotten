TWO WORLDS, DIFFERENT CLOCKS

Why do things last on
the Moon but not on Mars?

On the Moon

  • No air means no wind or rain to wear the surface away
  • Days can reach about 120 °C (250 °F); nights fall below −170 °C (−275 °F)
  • Micrometeoroids and sunlight slowly age what remains
  • Footprints and wheel tracks can outlast generations

On Mars

  • Thin air, with winds strong enough to lift and move dust
  • Dust can dim solar panels and starve a rover of power, as it did Opportunity and InSight
  • At some sites, winter cold ended Phoenix's short season
  • Changing seasons bring changing sunlight

On the Moon, it's mostly time. On Mars, it's dust, cold and sunlight.

WHY LEAVE THINGS BEHIND?

Why leave
things behind?

Space is a costly road with no roadside stop. Each kilogram needs fuel to leave Earth, and a return journey asks for still more. Bringing a rover home would mean carrying another rocket to Mars and back. So missions carry home what matters most, like rocks, and leave their machines beneath another sky.

Apollo crews returned with 382 kg (842 lb) of Moon rocks. Tools, cameras and even a rover stayed behind, saving room and weight for the journey home.
